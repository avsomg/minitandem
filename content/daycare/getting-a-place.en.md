# Getting a daycare place in canton Fribourg

First find out how your commune handles registration. Then register as early as its rules allow, and add each registration to My applications.

## Your commune sets the rules

How you register and what you pay depend on the commune where you live. Some examples:

- **Fribourg (city):** one central waiting list for all the city's subsidised crèches, run by the city.
  - Who can register: only families living in the city, or moving there soon.
  - When: from the 3rd month of pregnancy.
  - What you list: only the crèches you want, for at least 3 half-days a week.
  - Keep it active: renew your registration every 4 months, or it is taken off the list. Refusing an offered place three times also takes it off.
- **Villars-sur-Glâne:** go through the commune's foundation, FAEF, for any crèche place. Pre-register online; the fee is CHF 50.
- **Gruyère district:** register directly with the crèche you choose. If the crèche has an agreement with Option Gruyère, the district's association of communes, your commune decides on its subsidy and pays it under the district's common rule.
- **Glâne district:** ask a crèche for a place and, at the same time, ask Glâne Région's Petite Enfance commission for the subsidy.

If your commune is not listed, ask its office how to register and whether it subsidises crèche places.

## Register early and keep track

- Several registration forms can be filled in before the birth, with the expected due date, for example in Wünnewil-Flamatt, Granges-Paccot and Attalens.
- Where crèches keep their own lists, ask each one how its list works.
- Add each registration to My applications: the place, the date you registered and, if there is one, the date you must renew by.

## How many places are there?

- At the end of 2025 the canton counted 88 crèches with 2,909 authorised places; a year earlier, 81 crèches with 2,706 places.
- The money the canton set aside for creating new places is used up.
- The canton's early-childhood strategy for 2026–2030 points to two problems: care is spread unevenly across the canton, and there are too few places for children with particular needs.
- In the city of Fribourg alone, the central list held 137 children in October 2022, unborn babies included, who were to start within a year.

## Kinds of care

Compulsory school starts for children who are four years old by 31 July.

- **Crèche (Kita):** open at least 20 hours a week, so that parents can combine family and work. Many also offer half-days. On the city of Fribourg's scale for subsidised crèches, a half-day costs 45% to 65% of the day rate.
- **Family day care:** a child is looked after at the home of a host family, by the hour, through a regional association.
- **Part-time groups:** playgroups, nature playgroups, garderies (short-hours care groups) and maternelles (preschool groups). They are mainly for time with other children and open in principle up to 4 hours in a row; nature playgroups, held outdoors, up to 5 hours.
- Playgroups, nature playgroups and maternelles are for children aged 2 to 4; garderies may also have a group for babies.

## Montessori, nature and bilingual care

This is how the crèches and groups in the Daycare tab describe themselves. Of those that describe their approach:

- **Montessori:** two settings of a Montessori school in Châtel-Saint-Denis, the crèche Brin d'Eveil and the jardin d'éveil Les Coccinelles, describe themselves as Montessori. A further 21 say they use Montessori elements, among them La Providence in Fribourg and Les Cerisiers in Villars-sur-Glâne.
- **Nature and forest:** 51 name nature or the forest as part of their approach, including 14 forest or farm playgroups.
- **Bilingual:** 16 describe their care as bilingual or multilingual, for example L'Arche des enfants in Fribourg and the Kita Sandkasten in Kerzers.

Use the approach filter in the Daycare tab to find them.

## When no place is free

- **Family day care:** contact famiya (Sarine and Lac districts). It keeps no waiting list; its coordinators look for a childminder who fits the family's needs.
- **A crèche in another commune:** some communes subsidise places in crèches outside their own territory. The city of Fribourg's list of subsidised crèches includes one in Givisiez and one in Villars-sur-Glâne. Châtel-Saint-Denis has agreements with crèches in Attalens, Bossonnens, Saint-Martin and Vaulruz.
- **An individual agreement:** if no childcare in Châtel-Saint-Denis has a place, the commune can sign an individual subsidy agreement with a provider the parents found. Marly pays a share, by income, for a crèche outside the commune when the commune's own childcare cannot meet the family's needs.
- **A crèche that sets its own price:** one crèche in Cordast, for example, charges a fixed day price and asks families to request any communal subsidy from their home commune. Ask your commune before you sign; the guide "What daycare costs" gives examples.
