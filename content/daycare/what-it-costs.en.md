# What daycare costs

Look for your commune in the examples below and try its calculator if there is one. Ask your commune before you sign a contract with a crèche.

## Subsidised care: the price depends on your income

- In subsidised care, parents pay according to their economic means.
- Each crèche sets its scale with the communes that subsidise it.
- The canton and employers pay a share only for care run by a public body, an association or a non-profit foundation.
- There is a minimum price. The top rate cannot be higher than the cost price once the canton's and employers' shares are deducted.
- The canton's reference grid (2014) gives CHF 18 a day, meal included, as the crèche minimum. Some scales start lower, as the examples below show.

### What "determining income" means

- Determining income is the figure your price is worked out from.
- The canton's grid starts from the net income on your latest tax assessment (code 4.910) and adds some items, such as 5% of your taxable wealth.
- If your tax is deducted at source, determining income is 80% of gross taxable income plus 5% of taxable wealth.
- The grid covers incomes from CHF 40,000 to CHF 150,000. At CHF 40,000 or less you pay the minimum; from CHF 150,000 you pay the top rate.
- You also pay the top rate if your gross assets are above CHF 1 million, or if the tax office assessed you ex officio.

## Examples: price ranges by commune

Prices are for a full day in a crèche unless stated otherwise. These ranges are for families living in the subsidising commune or region.

- **Fribourg (city):** CHF 18.00 to 86.55, lunch, snacks and nappies included. Scale from 1 August 2021; the city has published no newer amounts on its childcare pages.
- **Villars-sur-Glâne:** CHF 15.10 to 110.00, meal included. From 1 August 2025.
- **Matran and Avry:** CHF 18.00 to 106.20, meals included. From 1 August 2026.
- **Attalens:** CHF 10.00 to 116.10, meals extra. From August 2026.
- **Romont (Casse-Noisettes):** CHF 12.00 to 111.25, plus CHF 6.00 for the meal. From 1 January 2026.
- **Family day care (famiya):** CHF 2.00 to 7.35 an hour for a child below school age, from 1 January 2026, in partner communes that subsidise the care.

## Calculators

- **Fribourg (city):** a spreadsheet (Excel) on the city's childcare page.
- **Villars-sur-Glâne:** a spreadsheet (Excel) from the foundation FAEF that gives an estimate.
- **Glâne district:** online forms that work out your determining income, for information only.
- **famiya:** an online calculator estimates the hourly price; the exact price comes with the contract. It does not apply to self-employed people or people taxed at source: they contact famiya's office.
- **Gruyère district:** there is no calculator for parents. Option Gruyère's workbook is meant for crèches and communes, so ask your crèche or commune for an estimate.

## Without your commune's subsidy

- **City of Fribourg:** families from another commune in the canton pay the city's average cost price, less the shares of the canton, employers and the tax-reform fund. They ask their own commune for any subsidy.
- **Attalens:** parents from another commune in the canton with no agreement pay the maximum cost. Parents from outside the canton whose commune does not subsidise the place pay the full gross cost.
- **KidsCare, Bulle:** CHF 146 a day before any subsidy; CHF 123.25 after the cantonal subsidy, which all canton residents receive. Rates from 1 April 2024.

Some communes help with a crèche outside the commune:

- **Givisiez:** only for a justified placement, and only if the commune signed an individual agreement with that crèche beforehand.
- **Marly:** a share, by income, when the commune's own childcare cannot meet the family's needs.

## Tax deductions for the 2026 tax year

- **Federal tax:** the deduction for childcare by third parties is CHF 25,800 per child. It rises to CHF 25,900 from the 2027 tax year.
- **Cantonal tax:** up to CHF 12,000 per child. The child must be under 14 and live in your household. The childcare costs must be documented and directly linked to your work, training or inability to work.

## The new federal childcare allowance

- The federal childcare law, passed on 19 December 2025, has no start date yet.
- The planned allowance is at least CHF 100 a month for a child in care one day a week. It rises by CHF 50 for each extra half-day of care a week.
- As a rule it is paid only when both parents work or are in training.
- It is paid until the end of the month in which the child turns 8.
- It covers regular paid care in a private or public childcare institution in Switzerland and in a national language, including family day care run by an organisation with legal personality. Grandparents and nannies do not count.
